\begin{abstract}
Etzion, Vardy, and Yaakobi conjectured that, for prime $p$, every
conference matrix of order $p+1$, reduced modulo $p$, generates an
MDS code over $\F_p$. We refute their Conjecture~5 at $p=29$.
An explicit symmetric conference matrix of order $30$ generates a
Euclidean self-dual $[30,15]_{29}$ code containing a word of Hamming
weight $13$; hence its minimum distance is at most $13$, rather than
$16$.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{The counterexample}

A conference matrix of order $n$ is a matrix with zero diagonal,
off-diagonal entries in $\{\pm1\}$, and
\[
  WW^{\mathsf T}=(n-1)I_n.
\]
Etzion, Vardy, and Yaakobi conjectured that, for prime $p$, the code
over $\F_p$ generated by any conference matrix of order $p+1$ is MDS
with parameters
\[
  \left[p+1,\frac{p+1}{2},\frac{p+3}{2}\right]_p.
\]
This is Conjecture~5 of \cite{EVY}; the statement carries the same number
in the openly available preprint version \cite{EVYpre}, whose concluding
section also lists it among the questions left open.

\paragraph{Status.}
Etzion, Vardy, and Yaakobi report that the conjecture was verified up to
$n=23$, for conference matrices arising from Paley's quadratic-residue
construction \cite{EVY,EVYpre}. The order $30$ treated here lies outside
that range, so what follows contradicts the conjecture and not the
reported verification: the conjecture is asserted for \emph{every}
conference matrix of order $p+1$, and one such matrix whose row span is
not MDS therefore settles it as stated. Nothing here decides the status
of the other conference matrices of order $30$, or of Paley's
construction at larger orders. Nor are the conjectured parameters an
empty target: \cite{EVY} notes that self-dual MDS codes of length $q+1$
over $\F_q$, $q$ a prime power, were constructed by Grassl and Gulliver
\cite{GrGu}. What fails at $p=29$ is the assertion that a conference
matrix of order $p+1$ always generates one.

\begin{theorem}
The code
\[
  C=\rowspan_{\F_{29}}(W)
\]
is a Euclidean self-dual $[30,15]_{29}$ code satisfying $d(C)\le 13$.
Consequently, $C$ is not MDS, and Conjecture~5 of
Etzion--Vardy--Yaakobi is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact integer multiplication gives
\[
  WW^{\mathsf T}=29I_{30},
\]
so $W$ is a conference matrix. After reduction modulo $29$, its rows
are mutually orthogonal. Thus $C\subseteq C^\perp$, and therefore
$\dim C\le 15$. On the other hand, the determinant of the leading
$15\times15$ principal submatrix of $W$ is
\[
  -8584878\equiv 21\pmod{29}.
\]
Hence $\rank_{\F_{29}}(W)\ge15$. It follows that $\dim C=15$ and
$C=C^\perp$.

Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
x={}&(25,5,26,12,0,6,19,16,0,22,5,1,26,23,14,
      \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{15})\in\F_{29}^{30}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct multiplication in $\F_{29}$ gives
\[
  xW=(1,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{14},
       15,6,0,8,27,0,18,14,0,15,4,11,20,17,19).
\]
Its support is
\[
  \{1,16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26,27,28,29,30\},
\]
so it has Hamming weight $13$. Hence $d(C)\le13$. By the Singleton
bound, an MDS $[30,15]_{29}$ code has minimum distance
$30-15+1=16$. Therefore $C$ is not MDS.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Provenance.}
The displayed matrix is reconstructed from representative~3 in
Spence's order-$30$ conference two-graph data file \cite{SpenceData}.
This provenance is not used in the proof; the matrix, minor, and
codeword above form a complete finite certificate.

\paragraph{Independent re-run.}
The computation was re-run independently, on a separate machine and by a
standalone program written against this manuscript, and it agreed: all
$43$ checks passed. The program and its transcript accompany this note.
Its mathematical inputs are
transcribed from this text and are nothing else:
the $30$ printed rows of $W$, the vector $x$, the printed product $xW$
with its support, the minor $-8584878$ and its residue $21$, the weight
$13$, the Singleton value $16$, and the parameters $[30,15]_{29}$.
Everything else it recomputes in exact integer and $\F_{29}$ arithmetic,
with no floating point anywhere. The checks cover the decoding of
the display and its zero diagonal, $\pm1$ off-diagonal entries and
symmetry; all $900$ integer inner products of rows, that is
$WW^{\mathsf T}=29I_{30}$, so that $W$ is verified to \emph{be} a
conference matrix of order $30$ rather than assumed to be one, together
with the independent consequence $\lvert\det W\rvert=29^{15}$; the
leading $15\times15$ minor $-8584878$ by fraction-free Bareiss
elimination and again by elimination over the rationals, and its residue
$21$; $\rank_{\F_{29}}(W)=15$; the equality $C=C^\perp$, obtained by
computing the $\F_{29}$ null space of $W$ and checking both containments,
the second of them also by solving $y=zW$ against the raw rows of $W$;
the product $xW$ recomputed entry by entry, with its support and weight
$13$; the same weight-$13$ word re-derived from the reduced row echelon
form of $W$ without using $x$ at all; and the Singleton value
$30-15+1=16$ together with the strict inequality $13<16$. Every number
in the theorem and its proof was reproduced.

Five limits of that re-run should be recorded, each of them noted in the
program's own output. First, the exact minimum distance of $C$ was not
determined. An exhaustive census of the codewords whose information
vector in the systematic coordinates has weight at most $5$, namely
$52\,531\,811\,444$ of the $29^{15}-1$ nonzero words, returns minimum
weight $12$; words of information weight $6$ or more were not
enumerated. The bound $d(C)\le13$ proved above is therefore correct but
not tight, which does not affect the refutation, since $13<16$ already.
Second, no census over the conference matrices of order $30$ was
attempted, and none is required: the theorem concerns the matrix
exhibited above and asserts nothing about the others. Third, the
provenance sentence was not checked, the program being self-contained
and fetching nothing. Fourth, four of the $43$ checks test the
program's own machinery rather than the paper, and cannot fail on a
well-formed input. Fifth, the checks are invariant under the sign
switchings $W\mapsto DWD$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(\pm1)$ whose $-1$
entries are confined to the coordinates where $x$ and $xW$ both vanish,
here coordinates $5$, $9$, $18$, $21$ and $24$: an orbit of $2^5=32$
displays. Every member of that orbit is again a conference matrix whose
$\F_{29}$ row span is a self-dual $[30,15]_{29}$ code containing the same
weight-$13$ word, so the theorem holds verbatim for each of them, and
within the orbit it is only the provenance sentence that singles out the
display printed above; the program prints its decoded matrix back so that
the display above can be compared with it character by character. An
independent re-run that agrees is evidence about the computation, not a
second proof.
