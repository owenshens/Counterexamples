## What is claimed

An affirmative answer to Problem 7.1 of Kinyon, Nagy and Vojtěchovský, *Bol loops and Bruck
loops of order pq*, J. Algebra **473** (2017) 481–512 (arXiv:1611.05785v3): for odd primes
`p > q` with `q | p^2 - 1`, every nonassociative right Bol loop `Q` of order `pq` has
`|Mlt_r(Q)| = p^2 q`, so the `p^3 q` branch of their Theorem 1.1(iv) is empty, and
`RInn(Q) ≅ Z_p`. The paper also strengthens their Theorem 1.1(v) from the Bruck loop `B_{p,q}`
to the whole family, with the module named:
`Mlt_r(Q) ≅ F_p^2 ⋊_{diag(w, w^{-1})} Z_q`.

**The decisive argument is a hand proof, not a computation.** Section 3 of the paper is five
steps of linear algebra covering all admissible `(p,q)` simultaneously, and a referee can read
it without running anything. The program below checks that proof; it does not carry it.

Two things a referee should hold the paper to, both stated in its Section 4:

* The theorem is **conditional on the exhaustiveness of KNV Theorem 1.1(ii)–(iii)**, the
 classification that supplies the normal form. That is assumed here and not reproved. If the
 classification were incomplete, so would the theorem be.
* Our reading of KNV's normal form and of their parameter set `Γ` is pinned by **reproducing
 their own statistic** `|Γ| = (p-q+2)/2` and by rederiving the right Bol identity from the
 transcribed product — not by an independent classification.

## The verification program

```sh
python3 verify.py
```

Python 3.9 or later, standard library only: no third-party package and no external data file.
All arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic in `F_p` and `F_{p^2}`; permutation groups are
enumerated as byte strings. No floating point and no randomness anywhere, so the output is
byte-stable. Runtime is about four minutes on a laptop, dominated by the brute-force census.

The program prints one line per check and a closing verdict, and exits 0 only if every check
passes. The recorded run reports **63 checks, all passing**:

 VERDICT: ALL 63 CHECKS PASS

It reads the objects exhibited in the paper — the parameters of Section 2, not a saved data
file — and rederives every quantity the paper asserts. What it checks, and how:

* **Section A** rebuilds the two cells the paper works by hand. At `(p,q) = (5,3)` it confirms
 `F_25 = F_5[z]/(z^2-2)`, that `w = 2+3z` has order 3, that `u = (1, 2+2b, 2-2b)`, that
 exactly `b ∈ {0,2,3}` are admissible, and that the two `Γ`-classes are `(1,2,2)` and
 `(1,1,3)` as printed; at `(7,3)` that `Γ = {1,3,4}` with the printed `u`-vectors, which is
 KNV's own list.
* **Section B** builds the multiplication tables **twice** — once from the affine form of
 Step 1, once by transcribing KNV's `θ`-form directly — and checks the two agree entry by
 entry. That equality *is* Step 1. It then verifies the loop axioms and the right Bol
 identity `((zx)y)x = z((xy)x)` exhaustively for every member of every cell with `pq ≤ 105`.
* **Section C** is the census. Over the 29 admissible cells with `pq ≤ 255`, the 534 right Bol
 loops of order `pq` in range split into 44 associative (29 cyclic + 15 nonabelian, the cells
 with `q | p-1`) and 490 nonassociative, and all 490 have `|Mlt_r| = p^2 q` **and**
 `dim R = 2`, with 0 anomalies. The two routines are independent by construction:
 `mlt_r` never reads the proof — it closes the group generated by the right translations and
 then *forces* closure by checking every one of the `pq` translations lies in the enumerated
 set, so an undercount is impossible — while `dim_R` computes only the dimension Steps 3–5
 predict. Its last check (C9) confirms the kernel of the shift action is elementary abelian of
 order `p^2` and consists of pure translations only (Step 2) — but on the 84 nonassociative
 members of the `pq ≤ 105` cells, **not** over the whole 29-cell census, because it builds the
 kernel elementwise. Every PASS line in `verify.output.txt` states its own range; the ranges
 are not uniform across the sections and should be read off the line, not off this summary.
* **Section D** checks each proof step as an identity rather than restating it. Three of the
 twelve checks (D2, D3, D4) run on the `pq ≤ 105` cells and the rest over all 29: the affine
 form of every right translation; `R_{(1,0)}^k = R_{(k,0)}`; the factorisation
 `R_{(k,l)} = n_{k,l} s^k`; that the rescaling `r_i = t_i u_i` turns conjugation by `s` into
 the plain cyclic shift (forward for `s^{-1} n s`, backward for `s n s^{-1}`); the decisive
 identity `x^{(k)} = γ(1+w^k) v_1 + (1-γ)(1+w^{-k}) v_{-1}` in `F_{p^2}`; the closed form
 `4γ(1-γ)(w^{-1}-w)` of the 2×2 determinant; that `R = W ∩ F_p^{Z_q}` is the *same* plane for
 every nonassociative `γ` in a cell; and that the shift on `R` has characteristic polynomial
 `X^2 - (w+w^{-1})X + 1`, i.e. eigenvalues exactly `w, w^{-1}`.
* **Section E** runs the controls, both polarities. The **forced positive** is the one that
 makes the 490 negatives mean anything: `u = (1,1,2)` at `(5,3)` meets KNV's loop conditions
 and is a genuine loop of order 15, but is **not** right Bol, and the same brute force returns
 `|Mlt_r| = 375 = 5^3·3` — the upper branch — with `dim R = 3`. So the decider is capable of
 returning `p^3 q`. Forced negatives: `γ = 1` at `(7,3)` gives the nonabelian group of order
 21 with `|Mlt_r| = 21 = pq`; `u ≡ 1` gives the cyclic group. Proved case: `B_{p,q}` at
 `γ = 1/2` at five cells, against KNV Theorem 1.1(v). The paper's hand determinant of the
 circulant of `(2,2,4)` is checked to be `-32 ≡ 3 (mod 5)`.
* **Section F** checks the counting claims (`|Γ| = (p-q+2)/2`, and the number of nonassociative
 members, over 91 cells with `q ≤ 13`, `p < 200`: 0 mismatches) and sweeps `dim R` over
 14,522 members of 159 cells reaching `p ≤ 400`, far past the brute-force range `p ≤ 83`. No
 third dimension value occurs.

The census figures the two programs report — 534 loops, 44 associative, 490 nonassociative, all
490 with `|Mlt_r| = p^2 q` and `dim R = 2`, 0 anomalies — agree. That agreement is between two
independently written programs, but not between two independent *definitions*: both build the

would not be caught by either. It is caught, if at all, by the statistic `|Γ| = (p-q+2)/2`

There is deliberately **no `witness.*` file and no witness cross-check**. The result is a
closure over an infinite family; the object the negation asked for — a right Bol loop of order
`pq` with `|Mlt_r| = p^3 q` — does not exist, so there is nothing to encode and nothing to
compare a second encoding against. The nearest equivalent is the pairing of `dim_R` against
`mlt_r` on all 534 census loops.

## Scope

The program's closing statement of what it does not cover, quoted from its output:

> NOTE SCOPE. What this program does NOT establish. (1) KNV Theorem 1.1(ii)-(iii), that every
> right Bol loop of order pq is isomorphic to Z_{pq} or to a member of the theta-family
> parameterised above, is NOT re-proved here: it is assumed, and every count in Sections C and
> F is a count over that family only. If the classification were incomplete the theorem would
> be too. (2) NOT RE-RUN: any loop outside the theta-family; any order other than pq -- and at
> order p^3 = 27 the analogous upper branch IS attained, so nothing here is a statement about
> right Bol loops in general. (3) The brute-force census is bounded at pq <= 255 (29 cells,
> p <= 83) and the dimension sweep at q <= 13, p <= 400; larger cells are covered only by the
> hand proof of Section 3 of the paper, which this program checks step by step but does not
> replace. (4) The control in Section E lies OUTSIDE the right Bol family, so it validates the
> decider, not the existence of a right Bol loop with |Mlt_r| = p^3 q -- the paper claims there
> is none. (5) Isotopism counts, KNV Conjecture 7.3 and the GAP LOOPS package are not touched
> by this program.

Three further limits belong to the paper rather than to the program, and are stated in its
Section 4. **Priority:** the GAP `LOOPS` package ships all left Bol loops of order `pq` for
`p > q > 2`, classified in KNV, so any individual value is one
`Size(RightMultiplicationGroup(...))` away for anybody and was very likely known to the
authors; only the uniform statement over all admissible `(p,q)`, the identification of the
module, and the case-split-free Step 4 are claimed new. We cannot exclude that the authors
knew the answer and posed Problem 7.1 as an invitation to a proof. **Access:** the Elsevier
text of KNV is paywalled, so no page diff was made between the e-print and the version of
record; the published World Scientific text of the order-27 companion returned HTTP 403 and
was read only in its arXiv form; MathSciNet was not consulted. **Statement numbering:**
"Problem 7.1" is the numbering of arXiv:1611.05785v3, whose paragraph carries no `\label` and
is identified by position (line 1095 of the single e-print `.tex` member); the problem is
reproduced in full in the paper, so what is answered does not depend on the label.
