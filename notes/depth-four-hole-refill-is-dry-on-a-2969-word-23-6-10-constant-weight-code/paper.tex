\title[Depth-four hole refill is dry]
{Depth-Four Hole Refill Is Dry on a $2969$-Word $(23,6,10)$ Constant-Weight Code}

\date{}

\subjclass[2020]{94B25, 94B65, 05B40}
\keywords{binary constant-weight code, $A(23,6,10)$, local exchange, hole refill,
exhaustive search, maximum clique}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Let $C_0$ be the $2969$-word binary constant-weight code \texttt{a23.6.10.2969H} of length
$23$, weight $10$ and minimum distance $6$, listed in full in Appendix~\ref{app:code}.
Lysenstoen \cite{Lys} proves that no exchange deleting at most three words of $C_0$ and
inserting more words than it deletes produces a valid $(23,6,10)$ code, and states the
depth-$4$ analogue as an open problem. We report an exhaustive verification that no such
exchange exists at depth~$4$ either. An elementary reduction, the one described in
\cite[\S XII]{BSSS}, cuts the $\binom{2969}{4}=3\,231\,108\,527\,626$ four-element deletion
sets to $4\,948\,069$, which with the smaller sizes leaves a space of $5\,094\,818$ sets; the
$14\,447$ of those that pass a necessary counting test are each decided by an exact
maximum-clique computation, and the remaining $5\,080\,371$ by that count alone. For every
deletion set $D$ in that space the largest pairwise-compatible set of insertable words has
size at most $|D|$, and that bound is attained for each of $|D|=1,2,3,4$. Since
\cite[\S XII]{BSSS} reports achieving $k$-optimality for $k$ in the range $2$--$5$ without
recording which codes it certified, we claim no priority at any depth; and since $C_0$ has
been superseded, no lower bound on $A(23,6,10)$ changes.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{The problem}\label{sec:intro}

Points are $1,\dots,23$ and a \emph{word} is a $10$-element subset of them, written as a
$23$-bit mask. Two words $u,v$ satisfy $d(u,v)=2\bigl(10-|u\cap v|\bigr)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compat}
 d(u,v)\ge 6
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |u\cap v|\le 7 ,
\end{equation}
and we call such a pair \emph{compatible}. A $(23,6,10)$ \emph{code} is a set of pairwise
distinct, pairwise compatible words; $A(23,6,10)$ is the largest size of one.

Fix
\[
 C_0=\texttt{a23.6.10.2969H},
\]
the $2969$-word $(23,6,10)$ code from Brouwer's table \cite{AEB}, reproduced in full in
Appendix~\ref{app:code}. The \textsc{sha}-$256$ digest of the in-order concatenation of its
$2969$ six-hex-digit words is
\[
 \text{\small\texttt{2cfc5d1bd8e2e61dc73a2ab31e3ec985d268d67cc635468912828c8a21b5cbfd}}.
\]
A \emph{candidate} is a word that is not in $C_0$; there are
$\binom{23}{10}-2969=1\,141\,097$ of them. The \emph{blockers} of a candidate $s$ are
\[
 B(s)=\{c\in C_0:\ |s\cap c|\ge 8\},
\]
the words of $C_0$ that $s$ is \emph{not} compatible with, and for $D\subseteq C_0$ we put
\[
 \Cand(D)=\{s\ \text{a candidate}:\ B(s)\subseteq D\}.
\]
So $\Cand(D)$ is exactly the set of words that may legally be inserted once $D$ has been
deleted. All of this is the vocabulary of \cite{Lys}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The statement under test.}
Lysenstoen proves \cite[the depth-$3$ theorem, \texttt{main.tex} lines 196--200 of
\texttt{arXiv:2607.19550v1}]{Lys}:
\begin{quote}
``There is no pair $(D,S)$ with $D\subseteq C_0$, $|D|\le 3$, and $S$ a set of weight-$10$
words, such that $(C_0\setminus D)\cup S$ is a valid $(23,6,10)$ code with $|S|>|D|$.''
\end{quote}
and then states, in a paragraph of its own carrying no number
(\texttt{main.tex} lines 221--225 of the same e-print):
\begin{quote}
``\textbf{Open problem.} The depth-$4$ analogue is unresolved: the model ($30{,}247$
candidates, ${\sim}888$k pre-seeded pairs) was not closed within the budgets tried (CP-SAT,
CBC, and SCIP each left it open), so we state it as an open problem rather than a theorem.''
\end{quote}
The target carries no statement number, so it is quoted in full; the line numbers are those of
the \LaTeX{} e-print.

\begin{remark}[the implicit disjointness clause]\label{rem:wlog}
Read literally both statements are false, the hypothesis $S\cap C_0=\varnothing$ having been
left implicit: take $|D|=1$ and let $S$ be any two words of $C_0\setminus D$. Insertion of
candidates only is the intended reading, since \cite{Lys} defines a candidate to be a
weight-$10$ word not in $C_0$ and the insertion variables of its model range over candidates
only. We therefore carry the clause as a hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $C_0$ be the code of Appendix~\ref{app:code}. There is no pair $(D,S)$ with
$D\subseteq C_0$, $|D|\le 4$, and $S$ a set of pairwise distinct words with
$S\cap C_0=\varnothing$, such that $(C_0\setminus D)\cup S$ is a $(23,6,10)$ code and
$|S|>|D|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\mathcal D$ be the family of all $D\subseteq C_0$ with $|D|\le 4$ that are unions of at
least one and at most four blocker sets; $|\mathcal D|=5\,094\,818$. For every $D\in\mathcal D$ the largest
pairwise-compatible subset of $\Cand(D)$ has size at most $|D|$; over the $4\,948\,069$
members with $|D|=4$ one has $|\Cand(D)|\le 6$. The bound $|D|$ is attained at each of
$|D|=1,2,3,4$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} implies Theorem~\ref{thm:main} through
Lemmas~\ref{lem:shrink}--\ref{lem:iff}.

\section{The reduction}\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:shrink}
Let $(D,S)$ be as forbidden in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and put
$D'=\bigcup_{s\in S}B(s)$. Then $D'\subseteq D$, the pair $(D',S)$ is again of that form, and
$D'$ is a union of at most $|D'|\le 4$ blocker sets. Hence $D'\in\mathcal D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each $s\in S$ is compatible with every word of $C_0\setminus D$ that survives beside it, so
$B(s)\subseteq D$ and therefore $D'\subseteq D$. Every $s\in S$ still has $B(s)\subseteq D'$,
so no word of $C_0\setminus D'$ conflicts with any inserted word, and the words of $S$ are
pairwise compatible because they already were; thus $(C_0\setminus D')\cup S$ is a valid code.
Finally $|S|>|D|\ge|D'|$, so the pair is again of the forbidden form. For the last clause,
take a subfamily of $\{B(s):s\in S\}$ of minimum size whose union is $D'$ and discard any
empty member; each remaining member contributes at least one point of $D'$ not contributed by
the others, so there are at most $|D'|\le|D|\le4$ of them. There is also at least one: $S\ne\varnothing$
because $|S|>|D|\ge0$, and no blocker set is empty, since a candidate with no blocker could be
adjoined to $C_0$, whereas $C_0$ is maximal --- a computed fact, recorded in
\S\ref{sec:computation}. So $D'\ne\varnothing$, as every member of $\mathcal D$ is.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:iff}
A pair as forbidden in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} exists if and only if some $D\in\mathcal D$
admits $|D|+1$ pairwise compatible members of $\Cand(D)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) Let $(D,S)$ be such a pair and $D'$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:shrink}. Then
$S\subseteq\Cand(D')$, $S$ is pairwise compatible, and $|S|\ge|D'|+1$; take any $|D'|+1$
members. ($\Leftarrow$) Let $S'\subseteq\Cand(D)$ be pairwise compatible with
$|S'|=|D|+1$ and put $D''=\bigcup_{s\in S'}B(s)\subseteq D$. Every word of
$C_0\setminus D''$ is compatible with every member of $S'$, the members of $S'$ are pairwise
compatible and lie outside $C_0$, so $(C_0\setminus D'')\cup S'$ is a $(23,6,10)$ code of size
$2969-|D''|+|S'|\ge 2970$, and $|S'|=|D|+1>|D|\ge|D''|$.
\end{proof}

So the question is a finite one about $\mathcal D$, and $\mathcal D$ is small: the reduction
replaces $\binom{2969}{4}=3\,231\,108\,527\,626$ four-element deletion sets by the
$4\,948\,069$ of them that lie in $\mathcal D$, a factor $6.53\times 10^{5}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:shrink}
uses no theorem of \cite{Lys}, only the definitions, so pruning with it cannot import an error
from the statement being tested. Neither the reduction nor the vocabulary is new. Already
\cite[\S XII]{BSSS} forms the bipartite graph with ``a blue vertex for every codeword of $C$ and
a red vertex for every vector not in $C$ that `bites' (lies at distance less than $d$ from) $k$
or fewer words of $C$'', and then, for each $k$-subset $S$ of $C$, searches ``among the red
vertices that are connected only to $S$'' --- in the notation of \S\ref{sec:intro}, the
candidates $s$ with $B(s)\subseteq D$.

Two further facts make $\mathcal D$ computable. First, every blocker set occurring in a member
of $\mathcal D$ has at most four elements, so the members of $\mathcal D$ are exactly the
unions of at least one and at most four elements of the finite seed family
\[
 \mathcal B=\bigl\{B(s):\ s\ \text{a candidate},\ |B(s)|\le 4\bigr\},
 \qquad |\mathcal B|=30\,117 ,
\]
carried by the $30\,247$ candidates with at most four blockers. Second, since every
intermediate union in such a chain is a subset of the final one, $\mathcal D$ is the closure of
$\mathcal B$ under the partial operation ``union, when the result still has at most four
elements''. Computing that closure is a fixed-point computation on a family of about five
million four-element subsets of a $2969$-element set.

\section{The computation}\label{sec:computation}

\subsection*{The blocker census}
The census of $B(s)$ over all $1\,141\,097$ candidates is the only step that touches the
whole candidate space, and it was done twice by disjoint methods. Method~1 enumerates, for each
$c\in C_0$, the $130+3510=3640$ words meeting $c$ in $9$ or in $8$ points and accumulates the
resulting multiset; this is sound because a word meeting $c$ in $10$ points equals $c$.
Method~2 uses that, for words of weight $10$, $|s\cap c|\ge 8$ holds if and only if $c$
contains one of the $\binom{10}{8}=45$ eight-element subsets of $s$: it indexes the
$2969\cdot45=133\,605$ eight-element subsets of codewords and then sweeps all
$\binom{23}{10}=1\,144\,066$ masks. The two methods agree on all $30\,247$ pairs
$\bigl(s,B(s)\bigr)$ with $|B(s)|\le4$, and Method~2, which visits every mask explicitly,
certifies in addition that
\[
 \#\{s:\ B(s)=\varnothing\}=0,
\]
i.e.\ $C_0$ is a maximal $(23,6,10)$ code. The distribution of $|B(s)|$ over the small values
is
\[
\begin{array}{r|rrrr}
 k & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\hline
 \#\{s:|B(s)|=k\} & 70 & 1178 & 6503 & 22496\\
 \#\{s:|B(s)|\le k\} & 70 & 1248 & 7751 & 30247
\end{array}
\]

\subsection*{Calibration against the source}
The counts $1\,141\,097$, $1248$, $7751$, $30\,247$ and the absence of zero-blocker candidates
are published in \cite{Lys} and are reproduced above; so are its three pair counts, which pin
our hex-to-bit reading of the listing: $5261$ conflicting pairs among the $1248$ candidates
with at most two blockers, and $43\,105$ and $887\,964$ pairs that both conflict and share a
blocker among the $7751$ and among the $30\,247$, the last being the ``${\sim}888$k'' of the
open problem.

\subsection*{The deletion space}
The closure described in \S\ref{sec:reduction} terminates after three rounds and
\[
 |\mathcal D|=\underbrace{70}_{|D|=1}+\underbrace{3567}_{|D|=2}
 +\underbrace{143\,112}_{|D|=3}+\underbrace{4\,948\,069}_{|D|=4}=5\,094\,818 .
\]
That the result is the least fixed point was verified directly: every member is the union of
the blocker sets it contains, and adjoining any one member of $\mathcal B$ to any of the
$146\,749$ members of size at most three produces nothing outside the family.

\subsection*{The sweep}
For each of the $5\,094\,818$ members, $\Cand(D)$ was assembled as
$\bigcup_{T\subseteq D}\{s:B(s)=T\}$. When $|\Cand(D)|\le|D|$ no subset of $\Cand(D)$ can have
more than $|D|$ elements and nothing further is needed; otherwise the exact maximum clique of
the compatibility graph on $\Cand(D)$ was computed by branch and bound under a cardinality
bound. No shard, no time limit and no heuristic cutoff were used; the largest instance has six
vertices. Over the $4\,948\,069$ members with
$|D|=4$,
{\setlength{\arraycolsep}{3pt}
\[
\begin{array}{r|rrrrrr}
 j & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6\\\hline
 \#\{D:|\Cand(D)|=j\} & 17\,027 & 936\,347 & 2\,791\,429 & 1\,189\,193 & 13\,911 & 162
\end{array}
\]
}
which sums to $4\,948\,069$; in particular $|\Cand(D)|\le 6$ always, and only
$13\,911+162=14\,073$ of the four-element deletion sets carry as many as five candidates. The
condition $|\Cand(D)|>|D|$ is necessary for a witness but not sufficient --- what
Lemma~\ref{lem:iff} asks for is a $(|D|+1)$-clique --- and exactly $14\,447$ members satisfy
it. Each of those received a full exact maximum-clique search, and that search, not the count,
is what decided it. The joint distribution of $\bigl(|D|,\ \omega(\Cand(D))\bigr)$ over those
$14\,447$ members is
\[
\begin{array}{r|rrrr}
 \omega & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\hline
 |D|=2 & 2 & 3 & & \\
 |D|=3 & 7 & 154 & 208 & \\
 |D|=4 & 16 & 753 & 6179 & 7125
\end{array}
\]
which sums to $14\,447$, and whose $|D|=4$ row sums to $16+753+6179+7125=14\,073$ --- exactly
the count of four-element members with $|\Cand(D)|\ge5$ obtained from the other table, so two
independently accumulated tallies agree. No entry lies to the right of the diagonal
$\omega=|D|$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:iff} this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and the two tables
together prove Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}.

\section{Attainment}\label{sec:attain}

Each deletion set counted on the diagonal $\omega=|D|$ of the last table admits a break-even
exchange of $|D|$ words for $|D|$ words; those entries are lower bounds on the number of such
sets, since only the $14\,447$ members with $|\Cand(D)|>|D|$ were clique-searched. We exhibit
one member of $\mathcal D$. Let $D$ consist of the words at $0$-based
positions $27$, $31$, $585$, $1333$ of Appendix~\ref{app:code}, namely
\[
 D=\{\texttt{294CE8},\ \texttt{29630D},\ \texttt{463A0D},\ \texttt{623607}\}.
\]
Then $\Cand(D)$ has exactly five members, whose blocker sets are as shown:
\[
\begin{array}{l|l}
 s & B(s)\ \text{(positions)}\\\hline
 \texttt{0B630D} & \{31\}\\
 \texttt{214D69} & \{27\}\\
 \texttt{423E0D} & \{585,1333\}\\
 \texttt{4A3705} & \{1333\}\\
 \texttt{4E3305} & \{585\}
\end{array}
\]
so $|\Cand(D)|=5>4=|D|$ and $D$ is one of the $14\,447$ live members. The ten pairwise
intersections inside $\Cand(D)$ are
\[
\begin{array}{l|rrrrr}
 |\,\cdot\cap\cdot\,| & \texttt{0B630D} & \texttt{214D69} & \texttt{423E0D} & \texttt{4A3705} & \texttt{4E3305}\\\hline
 \texttt{0B630D} & - & 5 & 6 & 7 & 7\\
 \texttt{214D69} &   & - & 4 & 3 & 2\\
 \texttt{423E0D} &   &   & - & \mathbf{8} & 7\\
 \texttt{4A3705} &   &   &   & - & \mathbf{9}\\
 \texttt{4E3305} &   &   &   &   & -
\end{array}
\]
Every entry except the two in bold is at most $7$. By \eqref{eq:compat} the only illegal pairs
are $\{\texttt{423E0D},\texttt{4A3705}\}$ and $\{\texttt{4A3705},\texttt{4E3305}\}$; both
contain \texttt{4A3705}, so removing that one word leaves a $4$-clique and no $5$-clique can
exist. Hence the maximum clique is
\[
 S_4=\{\texttt{0B630D},\ \texttt{214D69},\ \texttt{423E0D},\ \texttt{4E3305}\},
\]
of size exactly $4=|D|$, and it is the unique one.

Deleting the positions listed and inserting the words listed now gives valid $(23,6,10)$
codes of size $2969$ at four depths:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
 k & \text{delete (positions)} & \text{insert}\\\hline
 4 & 27,\ 31,\ 585,\ 1333 & \texttt{0B630D},\ \texttt{214D69},\ \texttt{423E0D},\ \texttt{4E3305}\\
 3 & 27,\ 585,\ 1333       & \texttt{214D69},\ \texttt{423E0D},\ \texttt{4E3305}\\
 2 & 585,\ 1333            & \texttt{423E0D},\ \texttt{4E3305}\\
 1 & 27                    & \texttt{214D69}
\end{array}
\]
In each case the gain $|S|-|D|$ is exactly $0$ and the resulting code again has minimum
distance exactly $6$. The case $k=1$ is a direct consequence of the census: all $70$
one-element members of $\mathcal D$ have $|\Cand(D)|=1$, so each yields a $1$-for-$1$
break-even exchange and none yields a $2$-for-$1$.

\section{Scope}\label{sec:open}

Neither the move nor the reduction that makes it searchable is ours. \cite[\S XII]{BSSS} calls
a code \emph{$k$-optimal} ``if its cardinality cannot be increased (while maintaining the
minimal distance) by deleting $k$ words and adding holes'', gives the reduction quoted in
\S\ref{sec:reduction}, and reports that ``in the range of our tables we are able to achieve
$k$-optimality for values of $k$ ranging 2--5'', a range containing $4$. It records no list of
which codes were so certified, and its Table XVI, the listing of codes with no known
structure, passes from $(23,6,8)$ to $(28,6,5)$ with no $(23,6,10)$ entry. We have therefore
not determined whether that $1990$ certification covers the particular $2969$-word listing
$C_0$ of Appendix~\ref{app:code}, and we claim no priority for any layer of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: what this note supplies for $C_0$ is a complete and independently
checkable search, not a new method, and a reader should treat the $|D|=4$ layer as possibly
covered in \cite[\S XII]{BSSS}. Independently of that, the $|D|\le3$ layer of $\mathcal D$ ---
$70+3567+143\,112=146\,749$ members --- re-proves the depth-$3$ theorem of \cite{Lys} quoted
above, and within the $|D|=4$ layer \cite{Lys} proves a depth-unbounded statement excluding
every $S$ all of whose members carry at most two blockers.

$C_0$ has been superseded: \cite{Lys} proves $A(23,6,10)\ge 2979$, and Brouwer's table
\cite{AEB} recorded $A(23,6,10)\ge 2992$ when we read it on $30$ August $2026$. Nothing here
improves or weakens any entry of that table. Not examined: depth $5$ and above, and codes
other than $C_0$. One limitation is internal to the accompanying program: in its histogram of
$\omega(\Cand(D))-|D|$ over all $5\,094\,818$ members, the buckets $-2$, $-1$, $0$ are
upper-bound-derived rather than measured, because for the $5\,080\,371$ members with
$|\Cand(D)|\le|D|$ the quantity recorded is $\min(|\Cand(D)|,|D|)-|D|$ and no clique search was
run. Nothing in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} or \ref{thm:sharp} depends on that distinction, a clique
being unable to exceed $|\Cand(D)|$.

\section{Reproduction}\label{sec:repro}

The computation is determined by the $2969$ words of Appendix~\ref{app:code} and the
definitions of \S\ref{sec:intro}; there is no random choice, no external solver and no external
data anywhere in it. It is a single pass over the $\binom{23}{10}$ masks followed by five
million assemblies of $\Cand(D)$, of which the $14\,447$ that pass the counting test call for a
clique computation on a graph with at most six vertices. The attainment claims of
\S\ref{sec:attain} need no machine: the five words of $\Cand(D)$, their blocker sets and the ten
intersections are printed above.

The program accompanying this note does exactly that, in Python with no third-party package,
reading the listing of Appendix~\ref{app:code} as its only input and re-deriving every integer
printed in \S\S\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:attain} above, including the eight taken from
\cite{Lys}. It runs in about a minute and a half on one core and reports $39$ checks, all
passing, followed by its own list of what it does not cover.
